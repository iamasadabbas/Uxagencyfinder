A good brief does two jobs. It helps agencies decide whether they're a real fit, and it makes their proposals comparable. You don't need a polished document. You need clear answers to the questions below.

## How to use this template

1. Copy the blank worksheet below into your own document.
2. Answer every section. Write "unknown" where you don't know yet. That's useful information for the agency too.
3. Send the same brief to every agency you contact, so their proposals can be compared fairly.

## Blank worksheet

```
UX AGENCY PROJECT BRIEF

1. Product and users
   - What is the product (or planned product)?
   - Who are the main users? List each role (e.g. clinician, dispatcher, shopper).
   - Which industry or market is it in?

2. Problem
   - What problem are you trying to solve?
   - Why now? What triggered this project?

3. Key tasks
   - Which 3-5 user tasks matter most?

4. Existing evidence
   - What research, analytics, support data or usability findings exist?

5. Scope
   - What's in scope (e.g. discovery, UX, UI, design system, prototyping, testing)?
   - What's explicitly out of scope?

6. Constraints
   - Technical platform, integrations, regulatory or accessibility requirements.
   - Existing brand or design system that must be kept.

7. Research access
   - Can the agency speak to real users? How many, and how will they be recruited?

8. Decision makers
   - Who approves the work? Who is the day-to-day contact?

9. Deliverables
   - What should exist at the end (files, prototypes, specs, documentation)?

10. Ownership
   - Who owns the designs, research and source files after the project?

11. Budget basis
   - Budget range, or preferred commercial model (fixed, T&M, retainer).

12. Dates
   - Ideal start date, key milestones, hard deadlines.
```

## Fictional example (abbreviated)

> **This example is fictional.** It shows the level of detail that's useful, not a real project.

```
1. Product and users: "RouteLine", a web app for regional delivery dispatchers
   and a mobile app for their drivers. Logistics / last-mile delivery.
2. Problem: dispatchers miss delivery exceptions because alerts are buried.
3. Key tasks: assign routes; spot and resolve exceptions; message drivers.
4. Evidence: 6 months of support tickets; no formal usability research yet.
5. Scope: discovery + UX/UI for the dispatcher dashboard. Driver app later.
6. Constraints: React front end; existing telematics API; WCAG 2.2 AA.
7. Research access: 8 dispatchers across 3 depots, recruited by us.
8. Decision makers: Head of Product (approval), Product Manager (daily).
9. Deliverables: research summary, tested prototype, UI specs, component library.
10. Ownership: all files and research transfer to us.
11. Budget basis: fixed price for discovery; open to T&M for design.
12. Dates: start in 6 weeks; prototype tested within 10 weeks.
```
