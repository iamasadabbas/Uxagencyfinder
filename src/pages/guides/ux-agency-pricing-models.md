This guide explains how UX agencies commonly structure their pricing. **It doesn't give market-average prices.** We haven't researched current rates, and any figures below are labelled arithmetic examples, not benchmarks.

## Common models

### Fixed project price
One price for a defined scope.
- **Good for:** well-understood scope with clear deliverables.
- **Watch for:** the assumption list. Anything outside it becomes a change request.

### Time and materials (T&M)
You pay for the time actually spent, usually at a day or hourly rate per role.
- **Good for:** discovery-heavy or evolving work.
- **Watch for:** ask for estimates, caps and regular burn reports.

### Retained / embedded team
A team works for you on a monthly basis.
- **Good for:** ongoing product work alongside your engineers.
- **Watch for:** team continuity and notice periods.

### Subscription
A recurring fee for a capacity of design requests.
- **Good for:** a steady flow of smaller design tasks.
- **Watch for:** turnaround limits and what counts as one request. Subscription pricing doesn't translate into a project minimum.

### Discovery-only
A short, paid phase to research and define scope before committing to a full project.
- **Good for:** reducing risk before a large build.
- **Watch for:** make sure the outputs are useful even if you switch agencies.

## Labelled arithmetic example

> **Illustration only, not a market rate.** A T&M estimate of 3 people × 20 days × a day rate of *R* = 60 × *R*. If *R* were 500 (in any currency), the estimate would be 30,000. Use the agency's actual rates.

## What's often extra

- Participant recruitment and incentives for user research
- Development and implementation support
- Taxes, currency conversion and payment fees
- Scope changes after sign-off
- Ongoing support and maintenance after delivery
