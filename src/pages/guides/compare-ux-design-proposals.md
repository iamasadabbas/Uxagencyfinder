Two proposals with the same price can describe very different projects. Before comparing totals, check that each agency understood the same problem and is proposing comparable work.

## What to compare

| Area | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Problem understanding | Did they restate your problem accurately, or just repeat your brief back? |
| Research | Who will they research with, how many people, and how? |
| Team | Named people and roles, and how much of their time is committed. |
| Scope | What's included and what's explicitly excluded. |
| Deliverables | Concrete outputs, not just activities. |
| Assumptions | What are they assuming you'll provide (access, content, decisions)? |
| Implementation | Do they support developers, and how? |
| QA | How will design quality and accessibility be checked? |
| Ownership | Who owns files, research data and source after the project? |
| Change control | What happens when scope changes? |
| Pricing basis | Fixed, time and materials, or retainer? What's the day rate or assumption behind it? |
| Support | What happens after delivery? |

## Red flags

- A fixed price with no listed assumptions or exclusions.
- Case studies from your industry that don't say what the agency actually did.
- A team described only as "senior designers", with no names or time commitment.
- Research mentioned with no participants, method or schedule.
