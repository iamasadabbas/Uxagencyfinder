Portfolios are marketing. They can still be useful evidence, as long as you read them carefully. Use this checklist on any case study an agency sends you, including the ones linked from our industry lists.

## The checklist

```
PORTFOLIO / CASE STUDY CHECK

[ ] Named project      Is the client and product actually named?
[ ] Agency role        Does it say what the agency did, versus the client's team?
[ ] Work stage         Concept, prototype, pilot, or shipped live product?
[ ] User research      Who did they research with, and how?
[ ] Relevant workflows Are the tasks similar to yours (not just the same industry)?
[ ] Constraints        Does it describe real constraints (regulation, data, legacy)?
[ ] Implementation     Did they support or do the build? Is it still live?
[ ] Claimed outcomes   Are results attributed and sourced, or just percentages?
[ ] References         Can you speak to the client?
[ ] Gaps               What does the case study NOT tell you?
```

## Reading industry claims carefully

- **The same industry isn't the same job.** A financial-research tool isn't evidence of payments experience. A university website isn't an LMS.
- **A client logo isn't a project.** Ask what was delivered.
- **Prototypes and visions are real work, but they aren't shipped products.** Our lists label these where the source makes it clear.
- **Outcome percentages are agency-reported.** Treat them as claims unless the client confirms them.
