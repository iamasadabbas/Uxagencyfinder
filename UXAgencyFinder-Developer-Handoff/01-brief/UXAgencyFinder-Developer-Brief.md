## 1. Domain assessment

**uxagencyfinder.com fits this product well:** visitors come to find an agency, and curated niche lists are a clear way to help them do that. A useful finder does not need to begin with a questionnaire.

The domain contains 14 letters before .com, familiar English words, and no hyphens or numbers. It communicates its purpose clearly. Its main brand limitation is distinctiveness because the wording is descriptive. Recognizable design, useful selections, and an accountable publisher will matter more than the name alone.

| Factor | Assessment | Practical implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning | Clear agency-discovery task | Lead with niche selection and agency recommendations. |
| Audience | Buyers looking for UX help | Write for product leaders, founders, design leaders, and procurement teams. |
| Extension | Familiar .com | Use a consistent HTTPS host and brand spelling. |
| Brand distinction | Descriptive rather than highly distinctive | Build recognition through original assessments and useful specialist coverage. |
| Scope | Focused on UX agencies | Keep unrelated marketing, development, and freelance directories outside the initial scope. |
| SEO potential | Relevant name in a competitive market | Build strong niche pages; the domain wording does not establish ranking authority. |
| Existing authority | Unverified | Do not assume inherited traffic, backlinks, or clean historical use. |

The original domain research on 18 November 2026 did not return usable live-site content or a useful exact-domain indexed result. RDAP and archive records could not be retrieved in that environment. This revision carries those observations forward; it does not establish registration availability, ownership, registrar dates, historical use, backlink quality, or penalties. An exact-name search was not trademark clearance. Search volumes and difficulty were not measured.

If already owned, confirm domain control before deployment. If acquisition is needed, verify current availability/seller terms and historical use before purchase. These acquisition checks do not prevent design and specification work.

## 2. Product positioning

**Positioning statement:**

> Find the best UX design agencies for your industry.

A buyer should be able to arrive with a real market context, such as a telemedicine product, payments platform, online store, fleet-management system, property platform, learning product, robot-control interface, or AI platform, and find agencies with relevant documented work.

The core journey:

1. Choose an industry.
2. Read its agency comparison and editorial assessments.
3. Inspect relevant industry projects and the agency's contribution.
4. Open profiles and contact appropriate agencies directly.

“Best” means a reasoned editorial selection against the published industry criteria. It is not proof that every agency in the market was tested or that the first-listed agency suits every project.

Initial measurement market: US English, with international visitors welcome. This is a planning choice, not measured geographic keyword demand.

## 3. Competition and editorial opportunity

The earlier research inspected Clutch, DesignRush, UXAgencies.com, and UXDesignAgencies.com. They already cover agency discovery through combinations of reviews, filters, specialist lists, profiles, and matching. Their visible features are not independently audited research methods. [R1-R4]

The site's editorial value should come from understanding the industry decision. A healthcare buyer needs to distinguish clinical software from benefits administration; a payments buyer needs relevant financial workflows; a logistics buyer needs operational and exception-handling experience; an education buyer needs learning and administration contexts.

Do not infer industry expertise from visual style, generic “SaaS experience,” a client logo without scope, or a marketing website for a company in that industry.

## 8. Eligibility, editorial order, and relationships

An agency is eligible for an industry or technology-market selection when a source identifies actual UX/product contribution in that niche. Robotics needs a relevant robotics product or tool. AI & ML needs actual AI-centric product/interface work; a client logo, generic AI service page, or marketing website for an AI company does not establish that eligibility.

**Owner-directed exception:** Include CorLab Tech at Logistics #5 as explicitly requested. Its reviewed pages describe relevant services but do not establish a named logistics client engagement. Record `selection_basis: owner-directed` and `evidence_depth: service-page`, and display the service-level qualification. This exception does not authorize automatic inclusion from generic capability claims or additional niche memberships.

Evaluate:

- Relevance of the named project and users to the industry.
- Clarity of the agency's actual contribution.
- Research, workflow, and delivery details that matter for that industry.
- Evidence stage and source limitations.
- What a buyer can usefully infer, and what remains uncertain.

Working-product claims remain attributed unless independently corroborated. Prototype, concept, research-only, and portfolio-overview work must retain their labels.

Each industry has its own editable editorial order. Positions are judgments based on the stated criteria and sources; do not create fake 9.8/10 scores, measured market leadership, or imported review aggregates. A source visit is not a client reference check or human editorial review.

Store position per industry placement, separate from the agency identity. The table, assessments, quick picks, and clean-page ItemList use the same published sequence. The complete directory remains alphabetical.

Record material ownership, client, paid, referral, and other relationships accurately. An operator's relationship with a listed agency is relevant to disclosure; do not invent a blanket independence statement. Sponsored placements, if introduced, are separately labeled and do not create evidence of industry expertise.

Missing public evidence is not proof that an agency lacks the capability. It means this selection cannot substantiate the claim yet.

## 9. Agency directory and profiles

The directory lists published agency identities alphabetically, with industry filter, name/alias search, A-Z/Z-A order, result count, and Clear all.

Industry filters use reviewed industry placements. Capability tags such as dashboard design, AI interfaces, SaaS, research, and design systems may describe recorded work, but do not automatically add the agency to an industry.

Render all directory/profile/source links in the initial HTML. Use fragment state for enhanced filters, for example `/directory/#industry=healthcare`, avoiding crawlable combination pages. Without JavaScript, readers can browse the directory and industry links. [R7-R8]

Each profile includes:

1. Agency identity, official site, and original industry-fit summary.
2. Published industry memberships and relevant subfields.
3. Named projects with users, problem, actual contribution, work stage, source, and buying implication. Service-level exceptions identify the source type and unknown delivery stage explicitly.
4. Sourced capabilities/product experience as supporting facts.
5. Commercial/location information only where researched.
6. Strengths supported by the evidence and questions before hiring.
7. Source notes, actual review metadata, corrections, and material disclosures.

Plan 21 substantive profiles from the recommended roster. Each must retain its supported niches and source qualifications. Publish after its original assessment, factual checks and editorial review are complete; do not replace missing copy with a thin profile shell.

Unknown commercial data distinguishes not researched, no current public figure found after checking, and a sourced figure with currency/basis/scope/date. Do not convert hourly or subscription pricing into an invented project minimum.

No copied third-party review summary or site-owned AggregateRating markup is required. [R11]
